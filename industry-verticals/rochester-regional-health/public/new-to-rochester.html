How to find a provider

Start with doctors who are accepting new patients

A move is a good time to choose a primary care home. These steps keep the search short, and they start with the people who have room for you.

1

Filter for accepting new patients

Open Find a Provider and look for the accepting-new-patients mark. The three doctors below already pass that filter.

2

Pick family medicine

Family medicine can see adults and children, which is handy when the whole household is new to town.

3

Check the practical details

Confirm the office is in-network, note the languages spoken, and see which neighborhood is easiest from your new address.

4

Call and book the first visit

Central scheduling at (585) 555-0100 will book a new-patient visit with a doctor who is accepting new patients.

Insurance

A few checks before the first visit

See if the practice is in-network

Plans can change when you change jobs or states. Before you book, confirm the doctor and the office are in-network for your current plan. The number on your member card, or the plan’s member portal, is usually the fastest way to check.

  • Ask scheduling to note the plan name you give them. This page does not list real insurers.
  • If a visit would be out of network, ask what that means for your share of the cost before you arrive.

Enrollment windows and your member ID

A move often opens a short window to join or change a health plan. If you are still choosing coverage, you can still call to ask about new-patient openings, then finish enrollment before the visit.

  • Bring the member ID card, or a clear photo of the front and back.
  • If the card has not arrived, the plan’s ID number and group number are enough to get started.

Your history

Transferring your medical records

You do not need a perfect file folder on day one. Bring what you have, and the office can request the rest.

1

Records-release form

Sign a release so the new office can ask your previous doctor for visit notes, problem lists, and test results.

2

Prescriptions

Bring a list of current medicines, or the bottles themselves, so refills can move to a pharmacy near your new home.

3

Vaccine history

A vaccine record, school form, or photo of an immunization card helps the team see what is already done.

For newcomers

Local tips while you get settled

  • Urgent care and lab. Learn the closest after-hours option before you need it. Birchmill Urgent Care on Wrenfield Road and Copperleaf Community Lab are fictional stand-ins for the kind of stop to look up near your address.
  • Pharmacy. Pick one pharmacy and ask them to pull prescriptions from your old one. Harborlane Pharmacy in this demo is not a real store.
  • Kids. Family medicine on this page can see children. If you want a pediatric-focused visit, say so when you call (585) 555-0100. Maplecourt Pediatrics, named here only as an example, is fictional.
  • First visit. A new-patient visit is for establishing care, not only for the day you feel sick. Book it while these doctors are accepting new patients.

FAQ

Questions people ask after a move

Are these doctors really taking new patients?

Yes. Claire L. Vandermeer, MD, Owen T. Calloway, MD, and Anika R. Desmond, MD are on this page because they are accepting new patients. Call (585) 555-0100 and ask for a new-patient visit with one of them. If a schedule fills, scheduling can look for another family doctor who is still accepting new patients.

How do I book if I do not know which doctor to choose?

Call the same central line, (585) 555-0100, and say you are new to Rochester and want a family doctor who is accepting new patients. They can match a location and a first opening.

What if my insurance card has not arrived yet?

You can still call. Bring the member ID number when you have it, and tell the office if you are inside a new-plan enrollment window after the move. This page does not name real insurers.

Can my children be seen too?

Yes. These are family medicine doctors. Mention each child when you book so the new-patient visits are set up together.
